Launch record

Every launch, wallet by wallet

When a new token opens on SimpleDEX, only its creator can buy for the first 60 seconds. The first block after that window is the first legal block. Guard.IAN's wallets aim for it. So do the bots.

This page lists each launch Guard.IAN has taken part in, read from the chain: how many of his wallets got into that block, and where they sat in it.

⚠
Test sizes. Each wallet now buys 0.01 XPR. GONDOR and LFQP were 1 XPR. Only IAN, the first launch, was real money (1,000 XPR per wallet). The size stays at 0.01 XPR until he passes the test below.

What changed along the way

  • XOXO and KWYS ran on the new sender timing. Both times all seven wallets got into the first legal block. At KWYS one of them was first in the block.
  • 2 October: a further fix to how the buys are sent went live. On the SimpleDEX testnet it put 5 of 5 test wallets in the first legal block (token 15, block 408825337). The next mainnet launch is its test.

The launch fleet

Seven wallets, all public, so anyone can check what they do. They buy at the first legal block, hold, and when the token graduates they claim, pair into the pool, and send the leftovers to the holding wallet. They never sell a community token and never pull liquidity.

Why seven: in the first minutes of a SimpleDEX launch each transaction can buy at most 5,000 XPR. The bots that take the first block use many wallets; so does he, in the open.

Who else is in that block

Since 3 March, 3,213 buys have landed in a first legal block. 3,191 of them, 99.3%, came from 32 wallets. His seven are not counted in those numbers.

How many people run those 32 wallets cannot be proven from the chain. We describe what the blocks show and name no one.

How the timing works

  • The contract checks the block's timestamp, not the clock.
  • The block stamped 60 seconds after creation starts being built about half a second early. That is why buys sent at about 59.5 seconds are legal.
  • A buy that arrives before that block opens is refused and dropped, not queued.
  • 21 block producers in the active schedule make the blocks, one every half second.
