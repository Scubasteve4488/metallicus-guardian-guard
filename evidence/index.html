How we label what we find

This is the standard the work is held to.

If something on this site breaks one of these rules, that's a fault. Tell us and we'll fix it.

Every answer is built the same way

  1. What we checked and found
  2. What it means
  3. What's worth a second look
  4. What we could not find out
  5. What would settle it
  6. Where it came from, and when

Four and five are the point. Anything can show you a green tick.

The labels

LabelMeansDoes not mean
CheckedRead from the source, with the time noted.That it's still true now.
Worth a lookSomething you'd want to see.That anyone did anything wrong.
Couldn't checkWe tried and got nothing.That it isn't there.
ContradictedA claim doesn't match the evidence.That anyone lied.
UnknownNothing could answer it.Anything at all.

Rules on every report

  • A failed check is never shown as a zero.
  • Missing data never means nothing happened.
  • If a source only gave us part of the picture, we say so.
  • If two sources disagree, you see both.
  • A trust score or KYC badge never counts as proof of honesty.
  • No verdict about a named person, ever.
  • No calling an account a bad actor without pointing at what it did.
  • Paying never buys a kinder answer. Holding a token never improves one.

What a receipt holds

A receipt ID, the subject, the question, the time, every source and when we read it, what we found, what we couldn't check, and whether it's since been corrected.

Observations can be permanent. Conclusions stay changeable. We never write a verdict somewhere it can't be taken back.
