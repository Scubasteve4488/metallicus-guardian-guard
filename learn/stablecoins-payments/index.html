What is a Stablecoin?

A crypto token built to track a steady asset, usually the US dollar. One dollar stablecoin aims to stay worth about one dollar. It usually does, but it depends on what backs it, and stablecoins have slipped off their peg before.

Meet the Metal Dollar (XMD)

XMD is backed by a basket of other dollar stablecoins, such as USDC (Circle), PYUSD (PayPal) and USDP (Paxos), not by one issuer. You mint XMD by depositing one of those 1:1, and redeem it the same way, on Metal X. The Metal DAO reviews what goes in the basket.

Learn About XMD →

Send to a name, not an address

On XPR Network you send to an @username. Type the name, enter the amount, confirm with your face or fingerprint in WebAuth. It arrives in about a second, and there's no gas fee. Check the name twice. A sent transfer can't be pulled back.

Banks and the bigger picture

Metallicus builds payment and digital-dollar tools for financial institutions as well as for people. It is listed in the Federal Reserve's FedNow service explorer, the US instant-payment system. Institutions can also propose their own stablecoins for the Metal Dollar basket through the Metal DAO. For current partners and programs, read Metallicus's own news.

Minting: you deposit a basket stablecoin (USDC, PYUSD or USDP) with the Metal Dollar treasury and receive the same number of XMD, one for one. Redeeming is the same in reverse. Minting is not buying: buying XMD on a market is a separate trade at the market price.

What's in the treasury right now

This reads the public Metal Dollar treasury straight from XPR Network when you scroll here: which reserve stablecoins it takes, how much of each it holds, and how much XMD exists. Nothing is stored or estimated.

—XMD in supply
  • Turn on JavaScript to read the treasury live, or look it up yourself: contract xmd.treasury, table tokens, on any XPR Network explorer.

Balances are what the treasury account holds. This page reads them; it does not audit them, and it makes no claim about backing. Each reserve row also has a configured limit, maxTreasuryPercent: a setting in the treasury's own table. It is not a measured share, and this page can't show whether or how the contract enforces it. On 28 October 2026 the USDC (XUSDC) row's limit read 60% while XUSDC made up about 96% of the reserve tokens held, so read the balances and the setting as two different things. The switches "minting on" and "redemption on" are table settings too, not proof a transaction will go through. A failed read says "couldn't read", never zero.
