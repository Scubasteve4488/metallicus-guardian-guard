Videos are streamed from The ASL Dictionary on the Internet Archive, by the Center for Accessible Technology in Sign. Every item there is marked public domain. StandGuard does not host, alter or re-publish them, and is not affiliated with CATS or the Internet Archive.

The alphabet

Not here yet, on purpose.

On 5 October 2026 this tab briefly showed the A–Z fingerspelling alphabet and said the signs were made by a real person. That was wrong. The clips came from a public-domain motion-capture archive, and what you actually watched was a 3D character rendered from that data. It was taken down within the hour.

Rewording it to “3D character” would have been accurate and still the wrong thing to publish. Fingerspelling is read off a real hand — the exact shape, and the way it moves between letters. A rendered model smooths out the detail a learner needs, and they would not find out until they tried it with a Deaf person.

So the alphabet comes back when there is footage of a real signer, and not before. The dictionary tab works today because that footage already exists and is public domain.

Logged on the corrections page, where everything StandGuard has got wrong is kept.

How this is made

Never generated

Guard.IAN does not invent a sign and does not render one.

He plays recorded footage of a human signer, or he says nothing. An AI guessing at a sign teaches somebody something wrong, and a wrong sign is worse than no sign. A 3D model replaying captured motion is not a person either — that rule was learned the hard way and is written on the alphabet tab.

Nothing is watching you

No camera, no account, no record of what you look up.

If practice with a camera is added later, the video stays inside your own browser and never leaves your device.

Public domain, not borrowed

The dictionary clips are marked public domain by the people who recorded them.

Most ASL video collections are research-only, because Deaf signers consented to research and not to somebody’s product. Those are not used here, licence terms are not stretched, and no clip is re-hosted.

Corrections are written by people

Any feedback the agent gives is a rule a person wrote and approved.

Not a model’s guess about your hand.
