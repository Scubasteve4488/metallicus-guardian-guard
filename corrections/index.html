Corrections

If something here is wrong

Point it out. A wrong fact on this site, or in something Guard.IAN published, gets fixed in the open.

What has already been fixed is kept below, including the things nobody reported.

How it works

  1. Point at the exact claim. The sentence or the number, not "this page is wrong".
  2. It gets rechecked at the source, the real one, not our own notes about it.
  3. Old and wrong are kept apart. A number that was right once and has gone stale is a different problem from one that was never true.
  4. The result is marked: corrected, clarified, kept as it was, or couldn't settle it.
  5. The change shows. Nothing shifts quietly under someone who already acted on it.

What counts

Yes

A wrong fact. An old number shown as current. A claim with nothing behind it. Something listed as working that isn't. Art that breaks a project's own story.

Not really

Disagreeing with a judgement the facts support. Asking for something accurate to come down. Asking for something added that nothing backs up.

You'll still get an answer. It goes back to the facts, not to whoever argued hardest.

The log

Everything corrected so far, newest first. Things found in-house are logged the same as things somebody reported, because a mistake nobody caught is still a mistake.

Corrected 5 October 2026 SignGuard

SignGuard said the ASL alphabet was “signed by a real person”. It was a 3D model.
The claim
A fingerspelling page published that morning at /learn/signguard showed A–Z and 0–10 and described the clips as “signed by a real person”. The Learn index carried the same wording on its card.
What was actually true
The clips came from a public-domain motion-capture archive. The motion had been captured from a human signer, but the video was a 3D character rendered from that data in Blender. Each clip's own documentation file states the Blender version and the model name. There is no human footage in that archive at all.
How it was found
Nobody reported it. The site operator opened the page, watched one clip and saw a model. The licence had been checked before publishing; the video itself had not been watched.
What was done
The player was pulled the same day, within the hour. The page was replaced with a plain account of what had been published and why it was wrong. The Learn index card was corrected. The page was marked so search engines would not keep the old version.
Why it was not simply reworded
Changing the page to say “3D character” would have been accurate and still wrong to publish. Fingerspelling is read off a real hand — the exact shape, and the way it moves between letters. A rendered model smooths out the detail a learner needs, and they would not find out until they tried it with a Deaf person.
What changed because of it
A standing rule, now written on the page itself: captured motion means footage of a person. A 3D model replaying captured motion is not a person and is not shown to a learner. The alphabet stays empty until there is real footage. The dictionary that replaced it streams public-domain video of real signers recorded by the Center for Accessible Technology in Sign.

Nothing before this date is listed because nothing before this date had been logged — not because nothing was wrong. This page described how corrections work for some time before it carried a single one.
